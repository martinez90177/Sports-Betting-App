Palace marks

Three ways to keep the bar-chart reading while giving the silhouette a palace. All are flat rectangles in lapis — same construction as the current mark, no illustration.

Round 5 E3 with the status colours

Green and red are the fastest-read pair in sports and finance — a viewer knows what they mean before reading a word. The cost: the mark stops re-tinting with a user's accent, and red carries loss. These four trade that off differently.

F1 · Full status
Cleared green, short red, white line. Reads as the product's chart on sight — and as a betting app.
F2 · Green only
Fill still says cleared, hollow still says short — but no red in the brand. Lapis pennant anchors it.
F3 · Lapis palace, status keep
The building stays brand; only the keep and the wings carry status. Three colours is a lot at 20px.
F4 · Status, no line
F1 with the dashed rule dropped. Cleaner, but the palace loses the thing it was being measured against.

Round 4 Putting the line back in the palace

The product's own grammar is two devices: a dashed rule at the line, and bars that are solid when they clear it, outlined when they fall short. Both work without green and red — so the mark can borrow them and stay all-lapis.

E1 · The line
A dashed rule cuts the facade where the line sits. The single most product-specific device you own.
E2 · Solid and outlined
Cleared towers filled, short towers hollow — the app's hit/miss grammar, minus the colour.
E3 · Both devices
The line drawn, and the towers obeying it: the two that clear are solid, the two below are hollow.
E4 · E3 with lit roofs
D1's caps kept on the solid towers. Most detail of the set — check it at nav size below.

Round 3 A second tone across the towers

All four keep B3's triangle pennant and use only accent and accent-ink — or opacities of the accent — so the whole mark still re-tints with the user's hue. Nothing here introduces a colour outside the palette.

D1 · Lit rooftops
An ink cap on every tower. Light falling on the roofline — and it echoes the bar-cap in the graphs.
D2 · Receding wings
Wings step back in opacity as they step down. Reads as depth without a second colour.
D3 · Alternating
Tones alternate across the facade, flag inverted against the keep. The most graphic of the four.
D4 · Windows and plinth
Knocked-out windows plus an ink plinth under the keep. Richest large, first to break small.

Round 2 Sharpening the pennant keep

The rectangular flag was the weak part — it reads as a bar poking sideways. A true pennant shape fixes it, and the wings can carry a little more palace.

B1 · Triangle pennant
The college-pennant shape. Unmistakably sport, and it points forward.
B2 · Swallowtail
Heraldic rather than athletic. More palace, slightly less stadium.
B3 · Two-tone flag
B1 with the pennant in accent-ink, so the flag separates from the towers.
B4 · Turreted wings
B1 plus small turrets on the outer wings. Most palace — busiest at 20px.
